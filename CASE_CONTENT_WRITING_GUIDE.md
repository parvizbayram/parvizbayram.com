# Portfolio Case Content Writing Guide

This guide is for writing future portfolio/work case content. It is not a design or implementation guide, and it does not change existing pages.

Use it for:

- Case-study body content
- Section titles
- Captions
- Stats and result copy

Do not use it for:

- Navbar text
- Project intro metadata unless Parviz asks
- SEO titles/descriptions
- Existing published case rewrites unless Parviz explicitly requests them
- Layout, spacing, media order, or coding decisions

## Reference Blend

Future case writing should combine three sources of language:

- ONY-style branding case logic: strategic, concept-led, visual-system focused.
- AIC-style product case logic: task-led, structured, UX/product reasoning focused.
- Parviz portfolio voice: calm, visual, precise, intelligent, and not salesy.

The result should feel like design thinking made readable. It should explain why decisions exist without overexplaining what the visuals already show.

## Core Voice

Write with confidence, but avoid hype.

Good tone:

- Clear
- Observant
- Design-literate
- Compact
- Calm
- Specific
- Useful

Avoid:

- Loud agency marketing
- Empty words like "unique", "modern", "innovative", or "seamless" unless the sentence proves them
- Explaining obvious visuals
- Fake results
- Overly academic language
- Long paragraphs that slow the page down
- Writing as if every project solved the entire business

The writing should make the work feel more intelligent, not louder.

## Before Writing A New Case

Ask these questions first. Some will be necessary, some will not, depending on the case.

### Project Type

- Is this a branding case, product/UX case, campaign case, or hybrid?
- If hybrid, which parts are branding and which parts are product design?
- Should the writing separate those parts clearly or blend them into one story?

### Role And Credit

- What was Parviz's exact role?
- Should the text say "I", "we", or use neutral/passive wording?
- Were there collaborators, agencies, or studios that should be mentioned?
- Are there people, agencies, or credits that should not be mentioned?
- Should the case sound personal or more studio-like?

### Context

- Who is the client?
- What does the client/company/product do?
- What market, country, or audience matters here?
- What was happening before the project started?
- Why did the client need this work?

### Task

- What exactly was designed?
- Was the task naming, logo, identity system, website, app, campaign, motion, social content, or something else?
- Was the goal strategic, visual, usability-focused, conversion-focused, or awareness-focused?
- What was the most important design problem?

### Strategy And Concept

- What is the core idea behind the work?
- What inspired the logo, interface, visual system, or structure?
- Which design decisions need explanation because they are not obvious from the images?
- Which details are better left visual and not described?

### Product/UX Details

- Who are the users?
- What user problem did the design solve?
- What business goal did the design support?
- What was changed in the information architecture, flow, navigation, or interaction model?
- Were there technical, content, or client constraints?
- Are there research findings, UX decisions, or usability improvements worth mentioning?

### Results And Stats

- Are there verified results?
- What exactly do the numbers measure?
- What should be excluded from the result explanation?
- Are the numbers public and safe to show?
- Should the result be written as hard performance data or softer impact?
- If there are no results, should we avoid a results section entirely?

### Boundaries

- Is anything confidential?
- Are there claims we should not make?
- Are there awards, publications, or recognitions that are safe to mention?
- Should we avoid process details, rejected concepts, client limitations, budgets, timelines, or internal strategy?

## Branding Case Pattern

Use this style for identity, naming, logotype, brand systems, campaigns, and visual strategy.

Branding writing should usually move from context to idea to system.

Recommended structure:

1. Context
2. Challenge
3. Core idea
4. Design logic
5. System/application
6. Result or closing meaning

Not every case needs every part.

### Branding Context

Explain what the brand is and why the identity mattered.

Good pattern:

```text
[Brand] needed an identity that could express [business/brand situation] while remaining clear across [main applications].
```

Better than:

```text
We created a modern and unique identity for the brand.
```

### Branding Challenge

Name the design tension.

Examples:

- The brand needed to feel expert without becoming cold.
- The identity had to work across digital, print, and campaign formats.
- The name had to communicate the company's approach without becoming too literal.
- The system needed to be recognizable even when reduced to simple forms.

### Core Idea

Explain the central concept in plain language.

Good pattern:

```text
The identity is built around [visual/strategic principle], turning [brand idea] into a flexible system.
```

### Naming

Naming copy should explain the logic, not just the meaning.

Mention:

- Source words or references
- Why the name fits the brand
- How it sounds or behaves
- What it allows the identity to communicate

Avoid:

- Overclaiming that the name is perfect
- Long etymology unless it matters visually or strategically

### Logotype

Logotype writing should connect form to behavior.

Mention:

- Geometry
- Letter modifications
- Rhythm
- Contrast
- Personality
- Reproducibility
- How the mark works at different sizes

Good pattern:

```text
The logotype uses [specific formal decision] to make the brand feel [quality], while keeping the form stable across [applications].
```

### Color

Do not say only that a color is "fresh" or "modern".

Explain:

- What the color signals
- Why it fits the category
- How it behaves in the system
- Whether it creates contrast against competitors

### Visual System

Explain how the identity expands.

Mention:

- Grid or modular logic
- Motion behavior
- Image style
- Icons/illustrations
- Layout principles
- Campaign adaptations

The system should sound usable, not decorative.

## Product / UX Case Pattern

Use this style for websites, apps, services, platforms, UX/UI, dashboards, and product campaigns.

Product writing should usually move from task to user/business problem to design decision to effect.

Recommended structure:

1. Product context
2. Goal
3. User or business problem
4. Structure and flow
5. Interface decisions
6. System, motion, or responsive logic
7. Result or practical impact

Not every case needs every part.

### Product Context

Explain what the product is and what the user needs to do.

Good pattern:

```text
[Product/company] needed a digital experience that helped users [main action] while making [content/service] easier to understand.
```

### Goal

Make the goal concrete.

Examples:

- Make services easier to scan.
- Reduce confusion around complex information.
- Present the brand as more credible.
- Guide users toward a clear action.
- Combine multiple service categories into one understandable page.

### UX / IA

Use this when the structure matters.

Mention:

- How information is grouped
- Why the page or flow is ordered that way
- What the user sees first
- How complexity is reduced
- How navigation supports decisions

Good pattern:

```text
The page is structured around [logic], so users can move from [first need] to [final action] without losing context.
```

### Interface Decisions

Explain interface choices only when they affect understanding or behavior.

Mention:

- Visual hierarchy
- White space
- Cards/sections
- Motion
- 3D or illustrations
- CTA placement
- Responsive behavior

Avoid describing UI like a screenshot caption:

```text
There is a button and a large image on the page.
```

Prefer:

```text
The interface gives each service its own visual space, using [decision] to make the offer easier to compare and remember.
```

### Motion And Interaction

Mention motion only if it carries meaning, guides attention, or clarifies the product.

Avoid:

- "We added animation to make it dynamic."

Prefer:

- "Motion is used to make each service feel distinct without interrupting the page's reading flow."

## Hybrid Cases

Some cases include both branding and product design. In those cases, do not force one writing style across the whole page.

Use:

- Branding style for naming, logo, identity, campaign, color, typography, and visual system sections.
- Product style for UX, interface, website, app, structure, navigation, and user-flow sections.

Good hybrid order:

1. Brand context
2. Identity idea
3. Visual system
4. Product or website task
5. UX/interface decisions
6. Applications/results

The transition between the two parts should explain how the brand system became a product experience.

Example:

```text
The same visual logic was carried into the website, where it shaped the structure, page rhythm, and service presentation.
```

## Section Titles

Section titles should be short and useful. They should name the content without trying too hard.

Good section-title directions:

- Naming
- Logotype
- Visual System
- Website Structure
- Interface Logic
- Launch Phase
- Product Campaign
- Social Media
- Results
- Impact
- Motion System
- Brand Applications

Avoid:

- Decorative titles that do not explain the section
- Long sentence titles
- Generic titles like "Design", "Creative", or "Solution" unless the section truly needs them

## Body Copy

Use body text to explain invisible design logic.

Recommended:

- 1-3 paragraphs per body block
- One idea per paragraph
- Keep line breaks controlled when the design requires exact layout
- Use concrete nouns and verbs
- Explain cause and effect

Avoid:

- Repeating the project intro
- Writing the same claim in multiple ways
- Describing every image
- Making claims without evidence
- Turning every design decision into a dramatic story

Good sentence patterns:

```text
Because [context], the system needed to [design need].
```

```text
The logo is built around [principle], allowing it to [behavior].
```

```text
The interface groups [content] so users can [action] without [friction].
```

```text
To keep [quality], we used [decision], giving the design [effect].
```

```text
This made the system flexible enough for [applications] while keeping the brand recognizable.
```

## Captions

Captions should be quiet and useful.

Use captions when they:

- Clarify what the viewer is seeing
- Explain a specific design detail
- Identify a phase, format, or application
- Add context without becoming a paragraph

Avoid captions when they only repeat the image.

Good caption style:

```text
Logo construction based on modular book proportions.
```

```text
Service sections use distinct 3D objects to make each offer easier to scan.
```

Bad caption style:

```text
This is a beautiful logo presentation.
```

## Stats And Results

Stats should be used only when they are true, verified, and useful.

Before writing stats, ask:

- What is the exact number?
- What does it measure?
- What time period does it cover?
- Does it include paid reach, organic reach, influencers, partner pages, or third-party accounts?
- Can this number be public?
- Should any part of the number be explained in light grey and any part emphasized in white?

Good stats copy:

```text
1M+
Total reach in teaser phase
without organic participation
from regular users.
```

Avoid:

- "Huge success"
- "Massive engagement"
- "The campaign went viral"
- Any number that cannot be verified

If there are no reliable stats, do not invent a results section.

## Questions About What Not To Write

Before drafting a case, ask which of these should be avoided.

- Should we avoid mentioning collaborators or agencies?
- Should we avoid exact client metrics?
- Should we avoid timeline, budget, or scope?
- Should we avoid technical implementation details?
- Should we avoid rejected concepts?
- Should we avoid saying "we"?
- Should we avoid saying "I"?
- Should we avoid awards or publications?
- Should we avoid naming third-party clients or partners?
- Should we avoid describing strategy too directly?
- Should we avoid strong claims like "first", "only", "best", or "largest" unless proven?
- Should we avoid mentioning the client problem if it sounds negative?
- Should we avoid conversion, revenue, or performance claims?
- Should we avoid process details that are not visible in the final work?

## Common Mistakes To Avoid

- Do not write generic agency language.
- Do not describe visuals that already explain themselves.
- Do not turn every case into a long story.
- Do not make the client sound broken before the project.
- Do not overuse "solution".
- Do not overuse "modern", "bold", "minimal", "clean", or "unique".
- Do not claim impact without evidence.
- Do not mix branding and UX logic in the same paragraph unless the project truly connects them.
- Do not write captions that compete with main body copy.
- Do not add stats just because a case feels like it needs an ending.
- Do not make product cases sound like branding cases if the main work is usability, structure, or interaction.
- Do not make branding cases sound like UX reports if the main work is identity, meaning, and system.

## Practical Writing Workflow

1. Identify the case type: branding, product, campaign, or hybrid.
2. Ask the necessary project questions.
3. Ask the necessary "not to write" questions.
4. Collect verified stats only if they exist.
5. Decide the section structure.
6. Write the first draft in Parviz's calm portfolio tone.
7. Remove hype.
8. Remove anything already obvious from the visuals.
9. Check line breaks and text block length for the designed page.
10. Make sure every paragraph explains either context, logic, decision, or effect.

## Final Rule

If the image already says it, do not repeat it. If a design decision is invisible without context, explain it.
