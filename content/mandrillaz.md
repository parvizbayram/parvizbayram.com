# Mandrillaz

Project type: Branding and UX/UI

Role: Brand Designer and UX/UI Designer

Canonical URL: https://parvizbayram.com/mandrillaz

Mandrillaz is an AI-powered fashion generator that turns prompts into custom apparel. Users generate images with AI and order printed versions on a T-shirt, sweatshirt, or hoodie in one continuous flow.

Mandrillaz was built around a simple question: what if an AI image could become a real product?

The project combines generative AI, e-commerce, and curated artist collections. Users could write a prompt, generate an image, choose the result they liked, and apply it to a T-shirt, sweatshirt, or hoodie. The product was designed for people who want clothing that feels personal: creative shoppers, people looking for unique pieces, and anyone searching for a memorable gift.

The challenge was that there was no direct reference for this kind of experience. I had to build the brand and product logic from scratch, connecting different behaviors from AI tools, e-commerce, and creative communities into one clear system.

## Name And Logotype

The name Mandrillaz comes from the mandrill: one of the most distinctive primates in the world. Mandrills are known for their scale, vivid color, and large social groups. I used those characteristics as a metaphor for the brand: expressive, unusual, colorful, and built around a community of people creating visual statements through apparel.

In 2023, MidJourney was strongly associated with Discord commands, especially /imagine. I used that slash as the conceptual starting point of the identity. It was integrated into the letter M, but also designed to work separately as a compact brand symbol.

The logotype uses a massive, characterful sans serif form. It feels digital, but not neutral. Its weight and shape connect back to the mandrill's physical presence: strong, unusual, and hard to ignore. The logotype combines three ideas: the slash-command culture of early AI tools, the physical character of the mandrill, and the clarity of a digital sans serif font.

## Color

Pantone P 65-8C. R237 G17 B99. C0 M96 Y34 K0. #ED1163.

The main brand color is magenta, inspired by the mandrill's vivid facial color. Together with black and white, it creates a compact and expressive palette: bright enough for fashion, sharp enough for digital interfaces, and memorable enough for a new product.

Basic Sans was used as a clean technological typeface. Its soft details keep the interface approachable, while also echoing some of the mandrill's organic character.

## Digital Product

The main page was designed around the prompt bar. It was the starting point of the product: a place where users could describe an idea, choose a visual style, or use voice input to begin generating an image. I wanted the first screen to feel less like a store and more like a creative tool. Before choosing a T-shirt, sweatshirt, or hoodie, users first had to create the artwork.

The hero section also included custom drawn illustrations that explained how the product works before the user starts interacting with it. They helped make the idea clear quickly: generate an artwork, place it on apparel, and turn it into a real product.

After that, the page introduced the idea through the "Wear what you think" section. It showed generated artworks applied to T-shirts, sweatshirts, and hoodies, making the result feel tangible rather than abstract. On hover, users could also see the prompts behind the visuals, connecting the final apparel back to the idea that created it.

The page then moved into curated collections, where users could browse ready-made AI artworks and use them as a starting point instead of generating everything from scratch. This gave the product two entry points: creating your own image or choosing from a visual collection.

To make image generation easier, I added style presets. Instead of writing a long prompt to describe a visual direction like anime, hyperrealism, or pixel art, users could choose a style and let the system help them reach the needed result faster.

The prompt bar also included voice input: a faster and more accessible way to turn an idea into a prompt. It works well for anyone who prefers speaking over typing long descriptions, including users with disabilities who rely on voice as their primary input method.

## Challenge

The main challenge was keeping the entire journey - from AI image generation to a finished, customized product - in a single uninterrupted flow. Traditional e-commerce UX moves users to a separate product page once an item is selected, which works for browsing a catalog, but in our case it would break the user's creative momentum right after they've generated something they're excited about. The flow also needed to support full customization (apparel type, color, size, fabric, print side) without feeling like a detour, and let users save or share their result without starting over.

## Solution

I designed the flow as a continuous path: generate an image, pick the strongest result, then choose an apparel item with the generated artwork already shown on a T-shirt, sweatshirt, or hoodie. Instead of routing users to a separate product page, customization - color, size, fabric type, print side - happens in a sidebar within the same screen, so the creative decision and the product decision stay connected.

It also included a link-generation option, so users could save or share their generated product without starting again.

## Design System

I created a compact design system to keep the product consistent across desktop and mobile screens.

It included product cards, collection cards, prompt controls, buttons, product selectors, apparel customization states, sidebar components, generated-image result cards, and checkout-related interface elements. The system had to support both the creative part of the product and the practical e-commerce decisions that followed it.
